No Second Chances: The Summer Pact

A Kai & Joey One-Shot

Joey Winters was convinced that the universe was testing her.

Out of all the people her friends could have invited on this summer holiday, it had to be Kai Rutherford. The bane of her existence. The one person she had sworn to hate for the rest of her life.

And now, they were standing in the middle of a rented beach house, staring at the last remaining bedroom.

A single bed.

"You have got to be kidding me," Joey muttered, her eyes burning with frustration.

Kai ran a hand through his dark hair, letting out a low chuckle. "Believe me, Winters, I'm not thrilled either."

Their friends—the traitors—had conveniently disappeared to the beach, leaving them to sort out the sleeping arrangements. The rules were clear: if they couldn't get along, they would be forced to stay in the creepy, isolated cabin at the edge of the property. Alone. Together.

Joey took a deep breath, remembering the pact they had made just hours ago. Never forgive. Never forget. Never fall in love. But be civil.

"Fine," she said through gritted teeth. "We share the room. But I'm taking the left side of the bed, and if you so much as breathe in my direction, I will smother you with a pillow."

Kai smirked, crossing his arms. "Deal. But I sleep with the lights off. I don't care if you're scared of the dark."

"I'm not scared of the dark," she snapped.

The first night was a disaster.

They laid on opposite ends of the bed, a wall of pillows between them. The silence was heavy, but the tension was thicker. Every time Kai moved, Joey tensed. Every time Joey sighed, Kai groaned.

"This is ridiculous," Kai said finally, his voice cutting through the darkness.

"What is?" Joey asked, refusing to turn her head.

"We're adults. We can share a bed without acting like we're in a high school rivalry."

Joey snorted. "We are mortal enemies, Kai. This is exactly how we're supposed to act."

There was a pause. Then, Kai spoke again, his voice softer this time. "Why do you hate me so much, Joey?"

The question caught her off guard. Her chest tightened. She could feel the weight of the truth pressing against her ribs. But she couldn't tell him. Not now. Not ever.

"I have my reasons," she whispered.

The days passed, and the forced proximity began to chip away at Joey's defenses.

She noticed how Kai always made sure the coffee was ready before she woke up. She noticed how he would walk on the side of the road near the traffic, instinctively shielding her. She noticed the way his eyes softened whenever she laughed at his stupid jokes.

One evening, while the others were out by the bonfire, Joey sat on the porch, staring at the stars. Kai joined her, handing her a glass of wine.

"You've been quiet all day," he said, sitting beside her.

Joey took a sip, the warmth of the wine settling in her chest. "Just thinking."

"About?"

She looked at him, the moonlight illuminating the sharp angles of his face. She hated how handsome he was. She hated how her heart betrayed her.

"About us," she admitted quietly. "About how much I hate you."

Kai held her gaze. "You say you hate me, but you look at me like I'm the only person in the world."

Joey's breath hitched. She wanted to look away, but she couldn't. "You don't know what you're talking about."

"Then tell me," Kai said, leaning closer. "Tell me why you hate me. Because I've spent years trying to figure it out, and I'm still lost."

The truth bubbled in her throat. The secret she had buried for so long. The reason she had pushed him away.

"Because it's easier to hate you," she whispered, her voice breaking. "It's easier to hate you than to admit that I actually care."

Kai's eyes widened. For a moment, neither of them breathed.

Then, he closed the distance.

The kiss was soft at first, tentative. But when Joey didn't pull away, it deepened. The years of anger, the unresolved tension—it all melted away in that single moment.

When they finally broke apart, Kai rested his forehead against hers.

"No second chances," he murmured, repeating the rule she had set.

Joey let out a shaky breath, her fingers curling into his shirt. "Maybe... maybe we can make an exception."

Kai smiled—a real, genuine smile that made her heart race.

"I'm counting on it."

And under the summer stars, with the sound of the waves crashing behind them, Joey finally let go of her rules. Because sometimes, the person you hate the most is the one who ends up saving you.
