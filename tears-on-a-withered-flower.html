Tears on a Withered Flower

A Haesu & Taeha Story

The rain poured heavily outside the small cafe, mirroring the storm inside Haesu's heart. She stared blankly at the cup of coffee in front of her, now cold and untouched.

It had only been a week since her world collapsed. Mincheol, the man she had built her life around, had thrown away their marriage for a younger coworker. And now, all she had left were the ashes of her past and a mountain of debt suffocating her.

She felt hollow. Like a withered flower left out in the sun for too long.

"Excuse me, is this seat taken?"

Haesu looked up, her eyes red from crying. Standing in front of her was a young man, impossibly handsome, with dark eyes that held an intensity she couldn't quite place. He looked a few years younger than her, but his gaze felt heavy, mature.

She shook her head slowly. "No. Go ahead."

The young man sat down, but he didn't look away. He just stared at her with a strange mix of longing and desperation. It made Haesu uncomfortable.

"I'm Taeha," he said softly.

"Haesu," she replied out of habit, though she immediately regretted it. She didn't want to talk to anyone. "Do I know you?"

Taeha smiled, a sad, knowing smile. "No. But I know you. Or at least, I know the pain you're carrying right now."

Haesu's breath hitched. "Excuse me?"

"I saw you at the courthouse," Taeha confessed, leaning forward slightly. "I saw how he looked at that other woman, and I saw how you looked at him. Like you were watching a stranger destroy your life."

Haesu felt the tears prick her eyes again. She hated how easily this stranger saw through her. "I don't even know you. Why are you telling me this?"

Taeha reached out, his fingers hovering just above her hand, waiting for permission. When she didn't pull away, he gently touched her knuckles. "Because you deserve someone who chooses you, Haesu. Someone who would burn the world just to see you smile."

"You don't know me," she repeated, her voice shaking.

"I want to," Taeha whispered, his eyes burning with sincerity. "Let me be the one to show you that you aren't a withered flower. You're just waiting to bloom again."

Haesu looked at this young man—this stranger who somehow saw her broken pieces and didn't run away. And for the first time since her world fell apart, she felt something other than pain.

She felt a spark.

Days turned into weeks. True to his word, Taeha slowly became a constant presence in Haesu's life. He didn't push her or demand anything; he simply stayed. He would wait for her outside her workplace, bringing her warm tea when she looked exhausted. He helped her sort through the mountain of bills Mincheol had left behind, never once complaining.

Haesu found herself looking forward to their quiet evenings at the cafe. The heavy, suffocating weight in her chest began to lighten. She started to smile again, a real smile that reached her eyes. The withered flower was slowly blooming under the gentle care of the sun.

"Why are you doing all of this for me?" Haesu finally asked one evening, as they walked along the Han River, the city lights reflecting on the water. "You could have anyone, Taeha. Why me? I'm broken, I'm divorced, and I have nothing to offer."

Taeha stopped walking and turned to face her. The wind gently ruffled his hair. He took her hands in his, his gaze so intense it made her breath catch.

"You are not broken, Haesu," he said firmly. "You were hurt. There's a difference. And I don't want anyone else. I only see you."

He reached up, gently wiping a stray tear that had escaped her eye. "I told you before, I'm not just trying to catch you. I want to keep you. Let me keep you, Haesu."

Haesu looked at the young man who had seen her at her absolute worst and still chose to stay. The walls she had built around her heart finally crumbled. She wasn't just a withered flower anymore; she was ready to love again.

She let go of his hands, only to wrap her arms around his neck and pull him close. "Okay," she whispered into his shoulder, a genuine, happy sob escaping her lips. "Okay, Taeha. Let's bloom together."
